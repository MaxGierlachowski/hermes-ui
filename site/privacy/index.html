Privacy policy

Last updated: 19 November 2026

In short
  • The developer collects no personal data through the app: no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking.
  • Your chats, images and voice messages travel only between your phone and your own backend, end-to-end encrypted.
  • What the app stores, it stores on your device. You can delete it at any time.
  • This is an unofficial, open-source app. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Nous Research, the makers of Hermes Agent.

1. Scope

This policy covers the mobile app for Android and iOS (“the app”), the web version of the user interface hosted on these pages, and these web pages themselves. The app is a client for the open-source hermes-ui backend, which you install and run on your own computer next to Hermes Agent. That backend and Hermes Agent are under your control; the developer of the app has no access to them.

3. No data collection by the developer

The developer runs no server for the app. The app contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or attribution software, and does not track you across apps or websites. Nothing you write, record or attach in the app is sent to the developer.

When you download the app, Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) process data under their own privacy policies. The stores give the developer aggregated, anonymous statistics (for example the number of downloads) and, if you have allowed it in your device settings, crash reports. The developer cannot identify you from them.

5. Data exchanged with your own backend

After you pair the app with your backend (by scanning a QR code or opening a pairing link), the app exchanges with it whatever you use the app for: chats and their messages, images and voice messages you send, your answers to approvals and questions, search queries, read markers and notification settings. Your backend may also tell Hermes Agent and, if you use it, the Paseo daemon on your computer what you entered. The backend runs on your computer; where your data goes from there depends on how you set up Hermes Agent (for example which AI model provider it uses). That is outside this app and the developer's influence.

The connection is end-to-end encrypted (QUIC with TLS 1.3) between the app and your backend. Your backend admits only devices you have paired.

6. Connection: iroh relays and address lookup

The app connects to your backend over iroh, a peer-to-peer network library. Where possible it uses a direct connection. Until then, or when a direct path is impossible, the encrypted traffic passes through a relay server. Your backend decides which relay is used: by default the public relays operated by n0, the developers of iroh, or a relay you run yourself.

  • The relay operator can see the endpoint ids (random public keys, not linked to your name or device hardware) of your phone and your backend, their IP addresses, and the time and amount of traffic.
  • The relay cannot read the content: it only forwards encrypted packets.
  • To find each other, iroh may also publish and look up address information (endpoint id, relay, possibly IP addresses) through n0's discovery service, and may ask your router to open a port mapping for the direct connection.

These services are part of the connection between your own devices. The developer has no access to them and no contract with their operators. If you do not want to use n0's infrastructure, run your own relay and configure it on your backend (see the project documentation).

7. Notifications

  • Local notifications: while the app runs, it creates notifications on the device itself. Nothing is sent to a push service for this.
  • Android, “Stay connected in the background”: optionally a background service keeps the connection to your backend open and shows a quiet ongoing notification. It uses no additional services.
  • Optional push via your own Firebase project: if you build the app yourself with your own Firebase project and configure your backend for it, your backend sends notifications through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM). The app then receives a device token from Google and passes it to your backend. The notification contains only the chat title and a short label such as “Approval needed” or “Done”, and passes through Google's servers; Google's terms apply. The store version is built without Firebase.
  • Web version, Web Push: if you enable push in the web version, your backend sends notifications through your browser's push service (for example Apple, Google or Mozilla). Their content is encrypted for your browser.

8. Permissions

The app asks only when you use the feature:

  • Camera: to scan the pairing QR code and to take photos for a chat.
  • Microphone: to record voice messages.
  • Photos: to attach images to a chat (Android uses the system photo picker, which needs no storage permission).
  • Notifications: to tell you when a reply is ready or the agent needs you.
  • Local network (iOS): to connect directly to your backend when it is on the same network.

Photos, recordings and camera images are sent only to your own backend, and only when you send them.

12. Deleting your data

  • In the app: Settings → “Forget connection” removes the pairing and the cache from the device.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored, including its device key.
  • On your backend: revoke the device in the remote-access dialog; delete chats in Hermes Agent as usual. Both are on your own computer.
